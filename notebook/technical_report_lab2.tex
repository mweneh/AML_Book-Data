\section{Data Quality Audit}
% =============================================================

We ran a full diagnostic pass over the raw mobile money transaction data before any
modelling work began, covering missingness rates, duplicate detection, data type
validation, and correlation screening. Ten columns showed the most significant problems.

Apply a regex parser stripping commas, the \texttt{/-} suffix, and brackets, inferring sign from context. Zero parse failures were observed after applying this rule. \\

\texttt{txn\_time} &
Three incompatible datetime formats coexist in one column. &
Sample: ISO \texttt{2026-06-30 14:24}, UK \texttt{07/04/2026 11:21}, long-form \texttt{Jan 29, 2026 01:17 PM}. A single-format parse fails on two thirds of rows. &
N/A &
\texttt{pd.to\_datetime} with \texttt{errors="coerce"} across three sequential formats. Recovers all timestamps with zero residual NaT values. \\

\texttt{reg\_id} &
Same customer appears under multiple variants from case and whitespace differences in the KYC field. &
Raw \texttt{reg\_id} has 11,722 distinct values. After \texttt{.strip().upper()} this collapses to 3,991 unique customers, a 66\% reduction. &
N/A &
Canonicalise to uppercase, whitespace stripped, before any grouping or split. Ensures one customer maps to exactly one group. \\

\texttt{agent\_id} &
Missingness concentrated in three regions rather than spread randomly. &
Missing 13.69\% overall. Arusha 45.2\%, Jinja 45.1\%, Mwanza 44.8\%; all other regions 0.0\%. Fraud rate equal between missing and present rows (8.39\% vs 8.41\%). &

Missingness fully explained by observed \texttt{region} column (a legacy logger issue). A dedicated ``Unknown'' category is sufficient; the pattern is predictable from existing model inputs. \\

\texttt{device\_id} &
Uniform dropout with no detectable driver across any observed column. &
Missing 5.91\%, flat across all 10 regions (5.6--6.1\%). Fraud rate: 8.38\% missing vs 8.40\% present. &
MCAR &
Treat missing as ``Unknown'' category. More complex imputation adds no value and risks introducing artificial structure. \\

\texttt{gps\_lat} &
High missingness that no observed column can explain, suggesting dropout depends on the unrecorded location itself. &
Missing 36.21\% (66,464 rows). Rate uniform across all 10 regions (35.4--36.7\%), so \texttt{region} does not explain it. Remote/indoor transactions fail GPS capture. &
MNAR &
Create a binary \texttt{gps\_missing} flag. The absence of a GPS ping may carry predictive signal; imputing coordinates would fabricate geographic data. \\

Identical dropout pattern to \texttt{gps\_lat}. &

N/A &
Drop entirely before any feature engineering. Does not exist at real-time scoring time. See Section 3. \\

\texttt{settlement\_status} &
Categorical outcome written after the transaction lifecycle completes. &
Fraud rate by status: \texttt{reversed} = 100.0\%, \texttt{held} = 65.1\%, \texttt{pending} = 0.0\%, \texttt{settled} = 0.4\%. Settlement resolves hours to days after the fact. &
N/A &
Drop entirely. No settlement outcome is observable when a transaction is submitted for scoring. See Section 3. \\

\section{Leakage Assessment Memo}
% =============================================================

Two columns in the raw dataset carry information that does not exist at the time a
real-time fraud decision must be made. Left in the training set, they produce offline
metrics that dramatically overstate how well the model will actually perform.

\textbf{Background: the six causes of data leakage (Section 2.7.2).} The course
material identifies six mechanisms by which future or outcome-related information
contaminates model training: (1) including the target variable directly as a feature;
(2) fitting preprocessing steps on the full dataset before splitting; (3) including
post-event fields written only after the outcome is known; (4) allowing duplicate
records to straddle the train and validation split; (5) failing to group related records
(e.g., same customer) on the same side of the split; and (6) using proxy variables that
encode the label without naming it directly. Both leaks identified below fall under
causes 3 and 6.

\subsection*{Leak 1: \texttt{manual\_review\_score}}

\textbf{Where it occurs.} This numeric column is stored directly in the transaction ledger
alongside raw event data, making it easy to accidentally include in a feature matrix.

\textbf{How the leakage happens.} The score is generated by a fraud analyst who reviews a
transaction only after it has already been flagged as suspicious. It encodes the analyst's
conclusion about whether fraud occurred (Cause 3: post-event field) and functions as a
near-perfect proxy for the target label (Cause 6: proxy variable). Both causes apply simultaneously.

\textbf{Impact.} Including this column produces a cross-validated ROC-AUC of
\textbf{1.0000}. Removing it brings the score down to \textbf{0.6252}, a reduction of
\textbf{0.3748 AUC points}. Any model selected on the inflated metric would have been
chosen entirely on the basis of this one column.

\textbf{Governance recommendation.} The fraud review score should be stored in a separate
operational table with a strict write timestamp, never joined to the raw transaction ledger
used for model development. All feature store queries must enforce a point-in-time cutoff
and explicitly exclude tables written after the scoring timestamp.

\subsection*{Leak 2: \texttt{settlement\_status}}

\textbf{Where it occurs.} This categorical field sits in the same transaction table as the
raw input features and describes the final resolution of the payment.

\textbf{How the leakage happens.} Settlement is a downstream business process that
resolves hours or days after the transaction is submitted. The value ``reversed'' is only
written once a fraud dispute has concluded (Cause 3: post-event field). Because reversed
transactions correspond to confirmed fraud at a rate of 100\%, the field is also a
near-perfect class separator (Cause 6: proxy variable). At real-time scoring, the field
would show only ``pending'' for every new transaction, providing no information.

\textbf{Impact.} Even after removing \texttt{manual\_review\_score}, retaining
\texttt{settlement\_status} sustains a near-perfect AUC because the ``reversed'' category
creates a 100\% fraud-rate split on its own. The combined inflation from both columns
together is \textbf{0.3748 AUC points} (from 0.6252 to 1.0000). The joint effect confirms
that both columns must be excluded simultaneously.

\textbf{Governance recommendation.} Settlement status should be treated as an outcome
field, not an input field. It should reside in a separate post-settlement table that is
excluded from all feature pipelines by default. Access to outcome tables should require a
documented exception reviewed by the model risk team.

\section{Reproducible Machine Learning Pipeline}
% =============================================================

The pipeline is implemented as a single scikit-learn \texttt{Pipeline} object. All
feature engineering, imputation, scaling, and encoding steps run inside the pipeline,
which means they are re-fitted from scratch on each training fold during cross-validation.
This eliminates preprocessing leakage by construction.

\textbf{Architecture.} A custom \texttt{FeatureEngineer} transformer handles amount
parsing, datetime extraction, cyclical encoding, missingness flagging, and customer-level
RFM aggregation. It is followed by a \texttt{ColumnTransformer} that applies
\texttt{SimpleImputer} and \texttt{RobustScaler} to numeric features and
\texttt{SimpleImputer} plus \texttt{OneHotEncoder} to categorical features. A logistic
regression classifier sits at the end of the chain.

\textbf{Cross-validation.} \texttt{GroupKFold} with 5 splits groups records by the
canonicalised \texttt{customer\_id}. This ensures no customer's transactions appear on
both sides of a fold boundary, preventing Leakage Cause 5.

\textbf{Result.} The honest, leakage-free cross-validated ROC-AUC is
\textbf{0.6037 ($\pm$0.0073)}. For comparison, the contaminated model (both leaky columns
included) scored 1.0000, confirming the 0.3748 inflation documented in Section 3.

\textbf{Serialisation.} The trained pipeline is saved to \texttt{pipeline.joblib} using
\texttt{joblib.dump}. It can be reloaded with \texttt{joblib.load("pipeline.joblib")} and
applied to a new raw transaction table without any preprocessing steps outside the object.
